Built for the whole shop

One place for everyone who touches a sale.

At the counter

Scan or search, pick a customer, take cash, card or UPI (including cash+UPI split), print a tax invoice. Partial returns reverse the original sale exactly, with the right batch and a credit note.

In the back room

Receive against a purchase order, capture batch, expiry and input tax, count the shelf, transfer between stores. Expired stock is blocked and sold first-expiry-first-out.

In the office

Every movement posts to a double-entry ledger. Trial balance, margin, supplier balances, GSTR-1 CSV, shifts and tasks, and exports your accountant can open.

Modules

What a trading day needs.

Each row is implemented and covered by the repository's tests. The limits are on this page too, in Honest limits.

Point of sale

Barcode keyboard input, product search, customers, manager discounts, promotion codes, cash, card and UPI (including cash+UPI split), park a bag with optional stock hold, printable tax invoices, shifts with counted-versus-expected cash.

Catalogue

SKU and barcode uniqueness, packs, categories, prices, tax rates, HSN, reorder levels, local product photos or an image URL, and CSV import from the browser or the command line.

Inventory

Batches, expiry blocking, FEFO selling, adjustments at actual batch cost, transfers between stores, stock-count sessions that detect conflicts, and reorder and expiry suggestions that show their arithmetic (including per-supplier lead days when set).

Purchasing

Suppliers, multi-line purchase orders, invoice text paste into a draft PO, partial receipts with input tax, unique invoice numbers, replenishment drafts, a copy-only supplier message, and supplier returns against credits.

Pickup storefront

A public catalogue with product sheets, a bag that counts, and a pickup request that ends in a clear timeline. Staff mark orders ready or cancelled; collection links atomically to the sale.

Customers and loyalty

One company-wide directory with a consent flag, and one point earned per ₹100, reversed if the sale is returned.

The books

Stock and money cannot disagree.

A sale, a receipt, a write-off and a return each write to the stock ledger and to the double-entry journal in the same transaction. There is no end-of-day sync to forget.

Transactional

One commit, two ledgers

Quoted prices and discounts are checked again at commit. A retried checkout is idempotent and never consumes a promotion twice.

Reports

Answers, not exports

Revenue, margin, inventory value, supplier balances, trial balance and date-range profit, plus stock and sales CSVs with formula-injection protection.

Boundary

Not certified GST

Pilot tax invoices and GSTR-1 CSV are included. This is not e-invoicing, not live GSTN filing, and not a certified accounting system.

Security

Boring, careful defaults.

Sign-in

Hashed passwords, opaque HttpOnly sessions, CSRF protection, persistent throttle counters, and session revocation. Optional single sign-on with Zyvor Haven or any OpenID Connect provider, with PKCE, and only for people you have already added.

Access

Five roles with branch scope, checked on the server. Staff can be disabled and passwords changed by admins or by the person themselves.

Network edge

An Envoy edge with HTTPS, HTTP redirects, token buckets, body and header limits, timeouts, and private routes for backup and metrics.

No independent security review has been done yet. See honest limits.

Deploy

From a laptop to a cluster.

The same application, three ways to run it.

Local

Python 3.12 with SQLite. One command, no packages, no build step, nothing leaves your machine.

Compose with HTTPS

A standalone stack with Envoy terminating TLS, redirects, rate limits and private operations routes.

Kubernetes

Non-root pods, probes, default-deny network policies, data and backup volumes, an edge-only autoscaler and a scheduled verified backup.

Honest limits

What Zyvor Retail is not, yet.

Taken from the product's own release scope. A long feature list does not prove a commercial launch.

AreaWhere it stands
MaturityA pilot release with a working core. Start with a controlled pilot whose workflows match the implemented scope.
Tax and statutoryPilot tax invoices (INV/CN), CGST/SGST/IGST, purchase input tax and GSTR-1 CSV are included. Not certified GST software, not e-invoicing, not live GSTN filing, no period close or year-end. Not a certified accounting system.
Payments and hardwareTenders are recorded, not initiated. Cash+UPI split is supported. No payment gateway, credit sales, calibrated scales or fiscal printer drivers.
OfflineService worker caches the console shell; a loaded tab keeps a pending checkout for manual retry and a last catalogue snapshot for brief disconnect. It is not a full offline POS.
Ask your storeRead-only questions answered from your own records (including a journal tax summary that is not tax advice), with the records used shown. Built-in rules understand a limited set of questions; a language model you connect (off by default) handles free wording but can still be wrong, so every answer shows its data and flags figures it cannot find there. No customer details are ever sent, and it cannot change anything.
SuggestionsStatistical estimates from your own sales, not a trained model. They need about three weeks of history and do not know about promotions or festivals. Lead time defaults store-wide; a supplier’s lead days can override per product. Nothing is ordered or marked down unless a person submits it. Invoice paste and supplier messages are drafts only.
StorefrontPickup only: no online payment, accounts, delivery, reserved stock, order tracking or verified phone ownership.
SecurityNo independent review yet, no MFA of its own, no audit signing, no encrypted application fields. Optional OIDC SSO is available and off by default.
Single sign-onOptional and off by default. Only people already set up in Retail can sign in: it does not create users or set roles from the provider, and there is no single sign-out. Tested with Keycloak (what Haven runs), not yet with Entra ID, Okta or Google. Passwords still work alongside it.
Zyntra feedAn optional, read-only feed of stock, bills, settlements, supplier lead times, queue waits and open shifts for Zyvor Zyntra, on its own access token and with no customer details. Zyntra’s approved requests (a markdown, an undo, a purchase-order cancel) arrive as tasks for a manager: Retail never changes a price or an order by itself. Queue waits only exist if staff log them, targets start from a general shop’s and should be set from your own history, and it covers one branch at a time. Tested with a Zyntra build from October 2026.
ScaleOne application instance on SQLite. No application high availability and no PostgreSQL migration yet; container and cluster validation is still to be run in your environment.
Not built yetVariants, serial numbers, bins, recalls, RFQs, PO approval chains, three-way matching, e-Invoice IRN, e-Way bill, live GSTN push, payroll, rostering, franchise royalties, forecasting.

Questions

Straight answers.

Can I run my real shop on it today?

Treat it as a pilot. The core trading, stock, tax-invoice and ledger flows work and are tested, but certified GST filing, payment gateways and hardware drivers are not there. Pilot it alongside your existing process first.

Does it do GST or e-invoicing?

Pilot tax invoices (INV/CN), CGST/SGST/IGST and GSTR-1 CSV export are included. This is not certified GST software, not e-invoicing, and not live GSTN filing. Not a certified accounting system.

Is there AI in it?

Two features. Suggestions is a statistical forecast, not a trained model: it turns your sales history into reorder and expiry advice that shows its arithmetic. Ask your store answers plain-language questions from your own records. By default it uses built-in rules and makes no network call. If you point it at a language model you run or choose (any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, such as Zyvor Nuvora or a local Ollama), the model only picks from a fixed set of read-only report functions and writes up the result. It is off until you set it up, it never sees customer names or phone numbers, and it cannot change anything.

Can staff sign in with our identity provider?

Yes, if you turn it on. Retail supports OpenID Connect, built for Zyvor Haven (Keycloak) and standards-based, so a “Sign in with…” button appears on the sign-in page. Your provider proves who someone is; Retail still decides their role and store, and only people already added in Retail can sign in. Passwords keep working, and the hosted demo does not use it.

Does it work with Zyvor Zyntra?

Yes, if you turn it on. Retail can give Zyntra a read-only feed of your stock, sales, settlements, supplier lead times, queue waits and open shifts, with a ready-made Zyntra pack that shows which numbers miss target and ranks what to do about it. It can also suggest targets from your own sales history. When Zyntra proposes something and a person approves it there, the request lands in Retail as a task for a manager; nothing changes in Retail until a person acts on it.

Does it need the internet or a cloud account?

No. It runs on your own machine or server with Python and SQLite, and the interface uses system fonts and original vector art. No CDN, analytics or external service is called.

How many stores does it support?

Several under one company: per-branch records, a shared customer directory and catalogue, admin branch switching, and immediate transfers. Consolidation across legal entities or currencies is not built.

Where does the data live?

In a SQLite database on infrastructure you run, with a verified backup job in the Kubernetes deployment. Existing databases upgrade through numbered transactional migrations.

Can I see the source?

The source repository is private. Ask us what we can share with evaluators.
